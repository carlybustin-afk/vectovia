## Purpose
These guidelines define how First Line is presented across its website, software, documents and communications. They establish a consistent identity for a connected software platform serving Environmental Health and Public Health professionals.

## Core proposition
**Digital tools for the first line of protection.**
Practical, intelligent software that helps Environmental Health and Public Health professionals assess risk, make informed decisions and take action.

**Professional principle:** Built to support professional judgement — not replace it.

**Central concept:** ASSESS → DECIDE → ACT. Assess risk and situations. Decide using evidence, legislation, guidance and professional judgement. Act through interventions, records, notices, reports and other professional actions.

## Brand identity and logo
**Name and descriptor.** The customer-facing name is FIRST LINE. The descriptor is ENVIRONMENTAL + PUBLIC HEALTH. Use First Line in ordinary prose. First Line EPH is working, legal and domain shorthand; EPH should not dominate the visible identity.

**Construction.** FIRST appears in Deep Navy and LINE in Signal Teal. A vertical line and node device connects the wordmark. The descriptor sits below in smaller uppercase navy lettering; the plus symbol can be teal. The line and node motif expresses connection, progression, professional pathways, evidence to action, surveillance and decision making. Use it as a recurring visual device across the website and application.

**Practical use**
- Use the supplied logo artwork as a single asset and preserve its proportions.
- Place it on white or a quiet, light background with sufficient contrast.
- Keep surrounding text and graphics clear of the wordmark and descriptor.
- Keep the descriptor readable; avoid shrinking the full logo into small interface spaces.
- Do not stretch, rotate, add shadows or rebuild the wordmark using a substitute font.
- Do not add decorative effects, change the wording or place the logo over busy imagery.

**Production recommendations.** Future assets: vector artwork, a transparent PNG and an approved compact mark. Provisional clear space: one node diameter around the full logo. Confirm minimum sizes against final artwork. The raster reference includes blue, teal and green nodes; the agreed palette governs interface and document colours. Green has no specified code: keep it in the logo and don't introduce a green interface token.

## Colour system
Deep Navy for authority and hierarchy, Signal Teal for key accents, on a predominantly white canvas. Supporting colours should make information easier to understand.

| Colour | Hex | Primary use |
|---|---|---|
| Deep Navy | `#082B4C` | Headings, navigation and primary brand typography |
| Signal Teal | `#14A38B` | LINE, calls to action, active states and key highlights |
| Data Blue | `#287A8B` | Secondary functional and data accents |
| Field Mist | `#EEF7F5` | Soft backgrounds and section differentiation |
| Ink | `#172536` | Primary body text |
| Grey | `#647381` | Secondary and supporting text |
| White | `#FFFFFF` | Primary canvas |

**Application**
- Navy or Ink for long passages of text.
- Teal selectively, so primary actions and active states remain easy to identify.
- Field Mist for subtle grouping without filling every section with cards.
- Data Blue for supporting data and functional emphasis.
- Status information must be understandable through labels and symbols as well as colour.

**Readability.** Check contrast for each text/background combination at its actual size. Don't assume white text on Signal Teal works for small button labels. Navy or Ink text on a light surface is the preferred starting point.

## Typography and visual style
**Typeface.** The brand decisions do not specify a typeface. Use Aptos for editable Word documents. For the website and application, use a clear modern sans serif with strong readability; Inter is a candidate to confirm before the final design system. Keep the logo as artwork; don't recreate its lettering in the interface or document typeface.

**Hierarchy and layout**
- Clear headings, readable body text and generous whitespace.
- A consistent, limited set of font weights and sizes.
- Uppercase for the logo descriptor and short labels where appropriate, not long paragraphs.
- Aligned content, calm layouts, comfortable spacing.
- Tables, lists and structured work areas where they suit professional tasks.

**Personality.** Credible, professional, modern, calm, practical, intelligent and human. It should be credible to councils, Port Health, national agencies and senior public-sector decision makers while keeping the clarity of a modern software company.

**Imagery and motifs.** Use the line and node motif for pathways, connected evidence and progression through work. Prefer meaningful workflow and product visuals to generic stock photography. Keep diagrams simple, with readable labels.

**Design boundaries.** Avoid a generic government, medical-clinic, consultancy or AI-startup look. Avoid excessive gradients, glassmorphism, neon, excessive animation, robot imagery and layouts made entirely of rounded cards.

## Voice and approved messaging
Plain English: professional, concise and practical. Use practitioner terminology when it helps the reader, and explain unfamiliar terms.

**Approved core wording**
- Digital tools for the first line of protection.
- Practical, intelligent software that helps Environmental Health and Public Health professionals assess risk, make informed decisions and take action.
- Built to support professional judgement — not replace it.

**Workflow language**
- Assess — Structured tools to help professionals identify and evaluate risk.
- Decide — Bring evidence, legislation, guidance and intelligent decision support together.
- Act — Turn professional judgement into clear actions, records and reports.

**Ask First Line.** Describe it as source-grounded professional decision support. Keep AI explanations distinct from authoritative legislation and guidance, and make the actual source visible. Example prompts: "What legislation could apply in this situation?", "What evidence should I consider collecting?", "What powers are available under this Act?"

**Claims and product status.** Use "support professional judgement" and "relevant legislation and considerations". Never claim that AI makes professional decisions or gives definitive legal advice. Describe planned functionality as planned or in development until it's available. Don't invent customer numbers, testimonials, partnerships, credentials, statistics, company history or certifications. Don't describe First Line as GDPR certified, ISO 27001 certified or trademark cleared unless that status is established.

## Brand applications and product family
**One connected platform.** First Line is one modular SaaS platform. The public website introduces the business; the authenticated application is the product. Brand language and navigation should show a connected workflow with shared cases, evidence, assessments, decisions, actions and reports.

**Initial module names**
- Ask First Line + Knowledge & Powers
- Investigation Builder
- Evidence & Inspection Toolkit
- Report & Document Builder
- CIEH Portfolio Coach
- Vector Surveillance Toolkit

These are the intended initial group; individual build scopes decide what's delivered. Wider modules remain future possibilities.

**Plans and access.** Planned: First Line Free or Lite, First Line Professional, First Line Organisation. Free and paid access sit within the same modules, with centrally managed entitlements. Confirm the Free or Lite name before publishing pricing.

**Vectovia.** Specialist enterprise positioning for full vector surveillance programme management. Tagline: Guided Vector Surveillance. "A First Line product" is a potential endorsement to confirm. The lighter First Line Vector Surveillance Toolkit should preserve that distinction.

**Website and contact.** Public navigation: Home | Environmental Health | Public Health | Products | About | Contact, with Sign In leading to the application when available. Primary domain: firstlineeph.com (firstlineeph.co.uk to redirect). Public contact: info@firstlineeph.com.

## Consistency check before release
Logo proportions, agreed colours, readable typography, professional wording, source visibility and accurate feature status. Apply alongside the platform architecture and each build's scope.
